## POS publication decision proposal — RB-17

`LIVE` currently derives from paid subscription/entitlement; it is not proof that a new immutable configuration snapshot exists. If legacy inline media blocks `provisionAfterPayment`, payment and subscription remain authoritative, configuration may remain `LIVE`, no new `publishedJson`/version is written, the draft is preserved, `ready` is false, `effectiveConfiguration` can fall back to that draft, and access remains entitlement-gated. **Business impact:** an owner may treat the POS as operational even though a paid setup was never published; changes may be less explicit/auditable than an immutable snapshot. **Consistency impact:** lifecycle and readiness disagree; current status copy may confuse onboarding/support and operational expectations. **Customer impact:** cashier behavior could use a draft rather than a reviewed snapshot; blocking sales would conversely interrupt a paid business and risk lost transactions/entitlement expectations. **Recovery:** preserve the payment, draft and legacy reads; fix the unsupported legacy field only through owner-controlled replacement after an approved asset store exists, validate configuration, then have the owner publish a new immutable version. Do not auto-migrate/delete or silently change sale access. **Recommendation:** keep settlement, entitlement and sales access unchanged; represent subscription entitlement separately from publication readiness and present “Paid — setup/publish required” until a valid snapshot exists. This presentation recommendation needs owner approval before customer-facing behavior changes; any sales block requires an explicit separate commercial decision.

## Caller-email fallback decision proposal — RB-12

For `ORDER`, `createOrderPaymentRecord` requires the order ID, re-reads the order under its business, requires `PENDING_PAYMENT`/`UNPAID`, derives the payable amount from `order.totalKES`, and derives the merchant owner from the business; caller-supplied amount/business identity does not override these. It prefers the order's syntactically valid stored `customerEmail`; if missing/invalid it permits a syntactically valid caller `input.email` (trimmed, max 160 chars, basic syntax only). A missing valid value returns 400 before provider initialization. This fallback does **not** verify mailbox ownership or consent and is stored on the payment/provider initiation; it does not rewrite the order email. Booking/retry code may also use a synthetic `@customer.jata.local` address. Tenant, amount and payment-state checks remain server-derived; the fallback is not wholly order-derived. **Consequences:** typos/caller-controlled addresses may receive provider correspondence/receipts, diverge from the order's contact, or expose contact data to the provider; no proof exists that payer controls the inbox. **Recommendation for owner decision:** keep current behavior until an explicit policy is chosen; if retained, show/confirm the recipient and record its source, and clarify where receipts go. A stricter policy (require order email or persist a customer-confirmed address before initializing payment) would be a product change and requires tests/consent review. Do not silently change fallback, mailbox verification, customer messaging or AI payment-request dispatch.

## 2. Read-only legacy-media SQL: assumptions and limits

The query scans **public base tables** except `_prisma_migrations` for a literal, case-insensitive `data:image` substring in `text`, `varchar`, `char`, `json`, `jsonb`, and `text[]` columns. It uses psql meta-command `\gexec`, quotes generated identifiers/literals, returns column labels and matching-row counts only, runs in a `REPEATABLE READ READ ONLY` transaction with a 2-second lock timeout and 30-second statement timeout, then rolls back. Usage is psql-specific; the SQL itself does not connect or select a database—the shell-expanded `LEGACY_MEDIA_READONLY_DATABASE_URL` in its comment is only the proposed command-line convention.

It is not a complete media inventory: no other schemas, `bytea`, external stores, non-`data:image` encodings or other representations; substring matches can be false positives; per-column counts cannot identify a business or affected row; full scans may time out. It has not been PostgreSQL-validated. Run only with a read-only role against the approved isolated snapshot; do not raise timeouts or return raw values without a separate owner/DBA decision.

## 5. Caller-email fallback: consequences and decision

`normalizedPaymentEmail` trims and applies a syntax check (length ≤160 and a basic `local@domain.tld` pattern); it does **not** verify mailbox ownership or consent. With an absent/invalid order email, a valid caller value can be used, stored on the payment record and sent to the payment provider; the order’s email is not thereby corrected. If neither is syntactically valid, payment-record creation returns 400 before provider initialization. Payment verification remains order/reference/amount/currency based, not email based.

Consequences: a mistyped/caller-controlled address may receive provider correspondence or receipts, expose contact data to the provider, and disagree with the order’s contact details; it is not proof that the payer controls that inbox. Booking and order-resume paths also synthesize phone-derived `@customer.jata.local` values in some cases; these are placeholders, not real mailboxes, and provider acceptance/delivery is not assured. The owner should decide the accepted source, what customers are told, and whether verification is needed. Until then, preserve behavior; test only with mocked providers. Do not initiate a real payment or add/change AI payment-request dispatch.
