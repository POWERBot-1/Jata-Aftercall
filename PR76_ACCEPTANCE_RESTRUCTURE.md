## Workstream A0: source map and concrete hazards

**Existing ERP ledger must be evaluated before creating anything parallel.** `ErpAccount`, `ErpPeriod`, `ErpJournal` and `ErpJournalLine` already exist; `lib/erp/accounting.ts` has `postJournalTx` with balanced debit/credit validation, period checks, source type/id, transaction-aware writes and idempotency, plus reversal and financial-report helpers. ERP invoicing, expenses and procurement already use this ledger, and ERP procurement also writes the same POS stock movement table. A0 must assess whether that journal can serve POS, including non-ERP entitlements, account bootstrap, closed-period behavior, currency/minor-unit bounds, concurrency, tenant/branch access, legacy migration and immutable history. Do not assume it already covers POS/provider writers, and do not create a second competing ledger by default. Retrofitting or extending this existing journal must be compared with other approaches against the same independent test contract.

1. **Atomic event identity:** `PosPayment` has no direct expense or purchase ID; `PosCreditEntry` has optional sale/payment links but lacks a universal original/reversal ID. `recordExpense` writes both expense and payment, so summing both as cash out is double counting; `occurredAt` and payment `createdAt` differ. Resolve with explicit durable source linkage and tested migration, not time/amount matching.
2. **Provider bridge and units:** POS uses integer whole KES (`amountKES`); provider records use integer `amountMinor` (100 minor units/KES). `applyConfirmation`, `applyReversal` and `applyRefundedTotals` can create POS effects; a canonical event must deduplicate both representations and refuse or explicitly account for non-whole-KES values rather than silently rounding. Provider request/pending is not settlement.
3. **Inventory:** `PosInventoryMovement.delta` is a float quantity, not a historical money value; cost can be missing or change. Inventory valuation cannot be recovered reliably by multiplying old quantities by today's product cost. Specify historical cost/valuation and fail closed where it is missing.
4. **History:** sale status/refund fields are mutable; a capture made later is not a historical period-end state. Existing snapshots are immutable versioned records, not opening-balance ledgers. A safe cutover must explicitly identify provenance, unrecoverable balances and unsupported histories; never backfill invented facts or modify existing captures.
5. **Wallet semantics:** payment destinations/provider transactions are not automatically a stored-value liability. A product decision is required before claiming deposit/withdrawal balance support. Do not make up wallet transactions.
6. **Time and order:** `createdAt`, `occurredAt`, `paidAt`, `completedAt`, provider events and actual entry recording differ; UTC periods use half-open `[start,end)`. Define late/backdated events, settlement dates and reversals; avoid assuming timestamps alone prove causal ordering.
7. **Concurrency and idempotency:** existing idempotency protections are scoped to individual actions. Canonical events must be written once, with uniqueness checked inside the same DB transaction as source effects and tested under retry/concurrency. The supplier-payment stale-read defect was reproduced locally on disposable PostgreSQL 18.4 using the Prisma WASM engine plus pg adapter: two simultaneous KES 60 payments both succeeded against a KES 100 balance (the regression test failed). The service now locks and revalidates the supplier inside the transaction; the same test, a rollback test and a tenant/permission refusal test passed. This does not establish request idempotency or complete accounting. `receivePurchase` reads the purchase before its transaction and updates received quantities from that snapshot; that separate concurrency risk remains unverified and unresolved.
8. **Drawer:** current `expectedCashKES` compatibility field is net cash movement, not the expected physical drawer balance; a verified opening float/count plus explicit adjustments is needed for the latter.

## First complete workstream A deliverable — acceptance contract

Before coding a journal, implement A0 as a verified source-event inventory and adopt a **single** event/period contract covering every live writer. Its acceptance requires: (1) a table of each public write entry point, transactional effects, idempotency scope, timestamps and links, including offline replay and provider callbacks; (2) a deliberate policy for unsupported legacy facts, minor units, cash opening and stock value; (3) independent fixture equations, migration and rollback plan; (4) reviewer confirmation that every supported operation is mapped or explicitly refused. This audit identifies sources and hazards, but the complete writer-by-writer mapping and approved policy are **not complete**, and no accounting implementation is claimed by this document.

Example independent end-to-end fixture (values in KES; amounts must be represented in minor units by the canonical engine): cash opening 200 + cash sale 120 − cash refund 40 + customer repayment 30 − supplier payment 50 − cash expense 10 = **250**; M-Pesa opening 0 + sale 80 − settled refund 20 = **60**. Customer opening 100 + credit sale 50 − repayment 30 − credit reversal 20 = **100**. Supplier opening 80 + bill 70 − repayment 50 = **100**. Stock opening 10 − sale 2 + return 1 + receipt 3 = **12**. Revenue/tax/valuation and wallet-provider effects must be specified independently in the approved A0 contract, not inferred from these cash equations. Real PostgreSQL acceptance must assert exact persisted rows, each independent result, source links and cross-period captures, including duplicated requests and tenant denial.

## Existing overlapping tests and why they remain

- `tests/unit/pos-refund-report-snapshot.test.ts`, `tests/integration/pos-refund-method-integrity.test.ts` and `tests/e2e/business-pos-journey.test.ts` 21c overlap on refunds: respectively pure calculation, route/transaction contract, and real PostgreSQL/capture behavior. None alone establishes complete period accounting; retaining distinct layers catches different failures.
- `pos-credit-age-fifo.test.ts`, `pos-credit-statement-safety.test.ts`, `pos-credit-period-projection.test.ts` and real-DB 21e cases overlap on credit arithmetic, reconciliation refusal and synthetic period projection. These are limited safeguards, not invoice allocation or A3.
- `pos-report-limit-refusal.test.ts`, `pos-refund-period-limit.test.ts` and real-DB cap scenarios check bounded-query refusal at different layers, not scalable pagination or full-volume reconciliation.
- `pos-profile-journeys.check.mjs`, `onboarding-authenticated.check.mjs`, `pos-photo-refusal.check.mjs` and premium screenshots overlap on viewport/UI: actual selected profile interactions, onboarding, storage refusal, and visual layout respectively. Transactions are not fully repeated at each viewport. Do not merge their green labels into a six-journey PASS.
- `payment-wallet-refunds-postgres.test.ts`, `paystack-refund-pos.test.ts`, provider webhook tests and POS refund tests separately test provider state, POS integration, authentication/idempotency, and till refunds. Their overlap is intentional; none proves actual provider settlement or complete consolidated accounting.
- Service-worker/unit tests, emulated offline notices and synthetic install event tests are component checks only. They cannot certify native install, OS standalone launch, worker upgrade or relaunch.
- `release:acceptance` tests only the offline inventory's safety/fail-closed behavior. It deliberately cannot satisfy the requested integrated release gate.
